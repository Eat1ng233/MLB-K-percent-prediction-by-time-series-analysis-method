# MLB Strikeout Rate Forecasting

This is my final project of time series analysis course in NCCU, using ARIMA model to do MLB strike out rate prediction. The result tells us that the K% of the next 10 years will stay at a high watermark and will not increase significantly.

The reason why I would like to choose this topic is that not only am I a die-hard baseball fan, but also urge to find if the change of the rule or training notion will cause a huge change in the K% metric, such as the ban of the splitball, the adjustment of pitching mound height, and the improvement of sports science. Therefore, I choose time series method to conduct my project since historical K% data is highly related to a time trend.

First, after using Lahman Baseball dataset to acquire relevant metrics, I conduct EDA to understand the trend and profile of the K%. Generally, even though K% is a upward trend from 1903 to 2025, but we could find out there were 2 significant decrease in the MLB history. After doing some research in the US professional baseball, I understand the first decrease from 1910 to 1930 is all about the prohibit of splitball, which make pitchers not to pitch the ball with insane movement, and increase the probability of batters hitting the ball. The second downward trend was between 1960 and 1980, which was related to the height adjustment of pitching mound. In addition, the basic statistics tell us the data profile with average, median, and quartile. I also draw a boxplot to describe the distribution of the data. 

Subsequently, I calculate ACF and conduct ACF test to ensure the stationary. I find out that the raw data is not stationary, so I do difference to satisfy the assumption. After that, I use ACF and PACF plot to determine the optimal lag order, which also told us a ARIMA model is more suitable to finish the project since I did not learn the time series method with multivariables. I chose ARIMA(0,1,1) and ARIMA(1,1,1) as candidate models after the plots observation. As a result, I finally chose ARIMA(1,1,1) with lower RMSE because the purpose of the model is prediction, though the AICc was relatively large.

After doing the model estimation and residual diagnose, I understand that the model is mostly meet the assumption and requirement, so I could take a look at the prediction result. Although the result tell us the K% of the next 10 years will stay at a high watermark, I still need to find the reason from other articles since I did not implement the analysis with other related variables. Surprisingly, the viewpoints written by some savants were close to my prediction result, which is described in the huge improvement of training methods, the implementation of data analysis, and the adaptation of the pitching clock. Overall, if the league rule does not change heavily, the K% metrics in MLB will not increase apparently at the same time.
